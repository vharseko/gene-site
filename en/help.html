How to use the site

Everything the family knows about its ancestors is gathered here: the tree, the story, a map, documents and old photographs. Nothing can be broken, so click freely. And if you remember something, write it down — that is what the site is for.

Tree

One person stands in the middle of the tree — their card has a frame. Above are parents and ancestors, below are children, beside them brothers, sisters and spouses.

  • Click a card — everything known about the person opens on the right, and the person stays visible in the tree.
  • To move to another branch, open the card and press “Put in the centre of the tree”. “+3 relatives” means the person has relatives who are not shown now.
  • Move the tree with the mouse or a finger, zoom with the + and − buttons, a pinch or the mouse wheel with Ctrl. “Fit” shows the whole tree.
  • Branches and surnames on the left: click a surname and the tree shows its branch.
  • “Horizontal” turns the tree sideways — handy on a wide screen.

The whole tree on one big diagram: the “Archive” tab, the “The whole tree on one diagram” button. It can be saved as a picture.

A person's card

The card gathers everything known about a person: how they are related, dates and places of birth, marriage and death, where they lived, their relatives, their life story, photographs, documents and relatives' comments.

Each date shows how we know it: from a document, from the family's account, from an age in another document. A number like S12 is a link to a document: click it and it opens with its transcription. A relative's name takes you to their card. Close the card with the cross or the key Esc.

How we are related

Every person's card has a “How we are related” block. It shows straight away how the person is related to the main person of the tree, and in the “Relation to” field you can choose anyone else — yourself, for example.

The site names the relation exactly — “second cousin once removed”, “great-aunt”, “half-brother”, the husband or wife of a relative — and shows the chain of people between you. Every name in the chain opens a card. If one of the links is not proven by a document, the site says so: “probable”.

Search

  • The magnifier at the top: start typing a name, a village or a word from a document — you get people, documents (by their transcriptions), places, chapters of the Story, leads and questions. Word endings, the pre-1918 Russian spelling and Latin or Cyrillic letters do not get in the way: “Kolodin” also finds the name written in Cyrillic.
  • The “People” tab: the full list with a search by name, relation and years; a “Calendar” of birthdays and anniversaries; “Surnames” — a page for every surname.
  • “People index” in the “More” menu — a page for every person; through it a relative can also be found by a search engine.
The most important

How to add or correct

There is a lot we do not know, and you may remember it: names, dates, where people lived, what they did, who is in an old photograph. Approximate is fine too (“I think, the late fifties”): we will note that it is a memory, not a document.

  1. Open the card of a person or a document — from the tree, the search or the archive.
  2. Press “Add details” or scroll down to “Comments and clarifications”.
  3. Write your name so that it is clear who you are (“Aunt Anna”). The site will remember it.
  4. Write what you know and press “Send”.

Suggest a correction

If a date or place is wrong, press “Suggest a correction” on the card: choose what to correct and write the correct value and how you know it. The keeper of the archive will see it and put it into the tree.

Answer our questions

The “Questions and discussion” tab lists what the family does not know yet. The “Reply” button attaches your answer to the right person.

Comments and proposals work when the site runs with its server. If there are no comment fields, write to the keeper of the archive: the address is usually in the site's header.

Archive

Documents and photographs

Everything the tree is built on: registers, certificates, letters, newspapers, manuscripts and family photographs. Search by name, place, year or a word from the description; the “Type” and “Line” buttons pick, for example, only photographs or only one branch. If a photo has a back, the viewer shows a “Back” button. Below is the “Research log”: where we looked and what we found.

Restored photos

Old photographs without creases and stains — in black and white and in colour, next to the original. The colours are the restorer's guess; the original remains the document.

Sources

Every archive, database and website where we looked for the family: what the source is, what was found there, and which documents and search-log entries belong to it. For parish registers there is a year × part table: what was read through, up to which entry, what is not on the film.

Corrections log

Every change in the tree: what was recorded before, what it is now, why, and which document it rests on.

The archive page offers the whole tree as a GEDCOM file — MyHeritage, Gramps, Ancestry and other genealogy programs open it.

Archive in numbers

How many people, documents and places have been gathered, where the documents come from, what confirms the people, how many open questions and corrections each family line has, births by century and the research log over time. The link is in the More menu.

Places and the story

Places — where the family lived. Every place has its own page: what was there, who of the family was born, lived or is buried there (by generation), which documents tell us so, what the place was called then and is called now, in which church's books to look for the registers, and a small map. The Places page shows the roads of the family's branches: places in the order of years, with numbered stops on the branch's map. All places at once are behind the link “All places on one map”.

Story — the story of the family. Read it from beginning to end like a book, jump along the timeline, or choose a place to highlight every passage about it.

Signs

  • A card with a frame — the person in the centre of the tree.
  • A dashed line and the ≈ sign — a probable link: we think so, but there is no document yet.
  • An empty “+ suggest” card — there should be a person here whom we do not know. Click it and write if you know who it is.
  • S1, S2… — documents of the archive; L1, L2… — leads: something was found but not checked yet.

Links and language

  • “Share” on the card of a person, a document or in the site's header copies a link. In Telegram, WhatsApp and other messengers it shows a photograph, a name and a description, and opens the same place of the site.
  • The address in the browser changes with what is open — it can be copied and sent too.
  • The language is switched in the header. Names and documents stay in the original.
  • “More”: “What's new”, this page, the “People index” and other pages of the site.

On a phone

  • The tabs at the top scroll sideways — there are more of them than fit on the screen.
  • Move the tree with one finger, zoom with a pinch.
  • The card opens from below over the tree. Close it with the cross or by tapping the darkened part of the screen.
  • On a phone, “Share” opens the phone's own share menu — straight into a messenger.

Living people

For living people the site shows only the name and the relation: no dates, places, documents or photographs — not on the pages, in the data file or in the GEDCOM export. Their pages are hidden from search engines. If you want more — or less — written about you, tell the keeper of the archive.

If something does not work

Reload the page, and if that does not help, reload it clearing the cache (on a computer Ctrl+Shift+R, on a Mac Cmd+Shift+R). If that does not help either, write to the keeper of the archive what you clicked and what you saw; a screenshot helps most.
